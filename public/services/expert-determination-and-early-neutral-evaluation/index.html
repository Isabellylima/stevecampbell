Expert Determination & Early Neutral Evaluation

Two proportionate alternatives to a full dispute: a binding decision on a defined technical question, or an independent, non-binding view of the merits.

Expert determination

In expert determination the parties agree to appoint an independent expert to answer a particular question or decide a particular dispute, and to be bound by the expert's decision. It is contractual — it exists only because the parties have agreed to it — and is usually fast, private and final, with very limited grounds for challenge.

The key feature is that the expert can use their own knowledge and experience to investigate the question, rather than deciding only on the evidence presented, as a judge or arbitrator must. That makes it well suited to narrow technical or valuation questions, for example:

  • Whether work has reached completion or a certificate should be issued
  • The value of a variation or a final account item
  • Whether remedial work is required, and its scope
  • Measurement and quantum disputes

Expert determination works best when the contract defines which questions can be referred, to whom, and on what timetable.

Early neutral evaluation

Early neutral evaluation (ENE) is a non-binding process. An independent evaluator, chosen by the parties, gives a reasoned assessment of the strengths and weaknesses of each side's case. The evaluator has no power to impose a settlement, but an objective view at an early stage often helps parties settle before costs escalate.

Without prejudice negotiation

Many disputes are best resolved by structured without prejudice negotiation, either at a meeting or in correspondence, with each party properly advised on its position. Steve can prepare and lead such negotiations for a party.

Frequently asked questions

Is an expert determination binding?

Yes, if the parties' agreement says so, which it usually does. The grounds for challenging an expert's decision are narrow — for example, where the expert has answered the wrong question or departed materially from their instructions.

What is the difference between expert determination and arbitration?

An arbitrator must decide on the evidence and submissions of the parties, and the process is governed by the Arbitration Act. An expert determiner may use their own expertise to investigate, and the process is governed only by the parties' contract.

Is early neutral evaluation binding?

No. ENE provides an independent assessment of the merits to help the parties negotiate. It does not impose a result unless the parties separately agree to be bound.
