Mediation

A confidential, facilitated negotiation that keeps the parties in control of the outcome — often the quickest route to a commercial settlement.

What is mediation?

Mediation is a flexible, confidential process in which a neutral mediator helps the parties negotiate their own settlement. The mediator does not decide the dispute. The parties remain in control of whether to settle and on what terms; a settlement becomes legally binding once it is written down and signed.

What is said in a mediation is "without prejudice" and cannot normally be relied on in later proceedings if the dispute does not settle.

Why mediation matters in construction disputes

  • It can take place at any stage — before proceedings, or alongside adjudication, arbitration or litigation
  • It is usually quicker and less expensive than a contested process
  • It allows commercial solutions a tribunal cannot order, such as future work or revised programmes
  • It can preserve working relationships between employers, contractors and consultants
  • The courts actively encourage it

The courts of England and Wales can order parties to engage in alternative dispute resolution (Churchill v Merthyr Tydfil County Borough Council [2023] EWCA Civ 1416), and an unreasonable refusal to mediate can lead to adverse costs consequences.

How Steve conducts a mediation

Steve chairs and manages the mediation, helping the parties identify the real issues, test their positions and explore options for settlement. He brings practical knowledge of how construction projects run — valuations, programmes, defects, variations — which helps conversations move beyond entrenched positions.

As mediator, Steve does not evaluate the parties' positions or advise on the merits of a settlement unless all parties expressly ask him to do so.

Steve also acts as an adviser or representative for parties attending mediations conducted by other mediators.

Independent appointment or party representation — never both. Steve may be appointed as the independent decision-maker in a dispute, or instructed to advise and represent one party. These are distinct roles. On any one matter he acts in one capacity only, and he will decline an instruction where there is a conflict of interest.

Frequently asked questions

Is a mediated settlement legally binding?

Yes, once the agreed terms are written down and signed by the parties. Until then, nothing agreed in the mediation binds either party.

Can we mediate while adjudication or court proceedings are running?

Yes. Mediation can take place at any stage, and parties sometimes agree to pause other proceedings while they mediate.

How long does a mediation take?

Most mediations can be arranged within a few weeks, and the mediation itself typically lasts one day, sometimes two for complex disputes.

Do we have to mediate?

Mediation is consensual, but courts can order parties to engage in ADR and may penalise an unreasonable refusal in costs. Many construction contracts also require negotiation or mediation before formal proceedings.
