Party Wall Surveying

Appointments and advice under the Party Wall etc. Act 1996 — for building owners, adjoining owners and fellow surveyors in England and Wales.

The Party Wall etc. Act 1996

The Party Wall etc. Act 1996 applies in England and Wales. It gives a building owner rights to carry out certain work affecting a neighbour's property, in exchange for obligations to give notice, protect the neighbour's property and make good damage. Its requirements are separate from planning permission and Building Regulations.

The Act covers three kinds of work:

  • Building on the boundary (section 1). A new wall at or astride the line of junction requires a line of junction notice at least one month before work starts.
  • Work to an existing party structure (section 2). Cutting into, underpinning, raising, thickening, demolishing or rebuilding a party wall, separating floor or party fence wall requires a party structure notice (served under section 3) at least two months before work starts. Loft conversions, beam insertions and chimney breast removals commonly fall here.
  • Adjacent excavation (section 6). Excavating within 3 metres of a neighbour's building or structure to a lower level than its foundations, or within 6 metres where the excavation would cut a line drawn down at 45° from the bottom of those foundations, requires notice at least one month before work starts.

The excavation category is the one most often overlooked by owners planning an extension.

What happens after notice is served

An adjoining owner can consent in writing or dissent. If they do not respond within 14 days, a dispute is deemed to have arisen. Under section 10 the owners then either appoint one agreed surveyor to act for both, or each appoints their own surveyor; the two surveyors select a third surveyor at the outset.

The surveyors resolve the dispute by making an award, which sets out what work may be done, how and when, and usually includes a schedule of condition of the adjoining property. Either owner may appeal the award to the county court within 14 days of service; otherwise it is conclusive.

The Act cannot be used to settle boundary disputes, and it cannot be used by a neighbour to block lawful work. A surveyor appointed under the Act acts impartially, whoever appointed them, and must be an individual rather than a firm.

Practical advice for building owners

  • Start early — notice periods are one or two months, and complex awards can take longer
  • Speak to your neighbours before serving formal notice; informed neighbours are less likely to dissent
  • Have your plans checked to confirm whether the Act applies and which notices are required
  • Where neighbours agree to an agreed surveyor, costs on small projects can be kept down
  • Make sure a schedule of condition is taken before work begins

The surveyors will normally award that their reasonable fees are paid by the building owner undertaking the work.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need a party wall agreement for my extension?

You need to serve notice if your work falls within section 1, 2 or 6 of the Party Wall etc. Act 1996 — building on the boundary, work to a shared structure, or excavation near a neighbour's foundations. If the neighbour consents in writing, no award is needed. If they dissent or do not reply within 14 days, surveyors must be appointed and an award made.

What does a third surveyor do?

The two party-appointed surveyors select a third surveyor at the outset. The third surveyor determines any matter the two surveyors cannot agree, and can also be called on by either owner or either surveyor to resolve a disputed point.

Who pays for the party wall surveyors?

The Act does not say so expressly, but surveyors will usually award that the building owner pays the reasonable costs, because the work is for the building owner's benefit.

Can a party wall award be challenged?

Yes, either owner may appeal to the county court within 14 days of the award being served. After that the award is conclusive and cannot be questioned in any court, except on limited grounds such as lack of jurisdiction.

Does the Party Wall Act apply in Scotland or Portugal?

No. The Party Wall etc. Act 1996 applies only in England and Wales.
