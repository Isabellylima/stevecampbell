Arbitration

A private, final and internationally enforceable route to resolving construction and property disputes. Steve accepts appointments as arbitrator and also represents parties.

What is arbitration?

Arbitration is a private alternative to court proceedings. The parties agree — usually in their contract, sometimes after a dispute arises — to refer disputes to one or more arbitrators, whose award is final and binding. In England, Wales and Northern Ireland arbitration is governed by the Arbitration Act 1996, as amended (including by the Arbitration Act 2025).

Arbitration is more formal than adjudication but more flexible than litigation. The parties can choose an arbitrator with relevant construction expertise, agree the procedure, and keep the proceedings and award confidential.

When arbitration is appropriate

  • The contract contains an arbitration agreement (common in JCT, NEC and FIDIC-based contracts, depending on the options chosen)
  • A final decision is needed, rather than an interim one
  • Confidentiality matters to one or both parties
  • The dispute is technical and benefits from an arbitrator with construction experience
  • The parties are in different countries and need an award enforceable abroad

International arbitration

Arbitration awards can be recognised and enforced in more than 170 countries under the 1958 New York Convention, to which both the United Kingdom and Portugal are parties. This makes arbitration a natural choice for cross-border construction and property disputes — for example, a UK-based owner or contractor working on a project in Portugal. The law that governs the arbitration depends on its seat; an arbitration seated in Portugal, for instance, is governed by the Portuguese Voluntary Arbitration Law (Law no. 63/2011).

Challenging an award

Rights of challenge are deliberately limited. Under the Arbitration Act 1996 an award can be challenged for lack of substantive jurisdiction, for serious irregularity, or — unless excluded by agreement — appealed on a point of law with the court's permission. In practice, parties should expect the award to be final.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between arbitration and adjudication?

Adjudication is a statutory, fast-track process for construction contracts, producing a decision that is binding only until the dispute is finally resolved. Arbitration is consensual — it needs an arbitration agreement — and produces a final and binding award with very limited rights of challenge. They are not interchangeable.

Is arbitration confidential?

Arbitration is private, and in English law parties are generally subject to duties of confidentiality, subject to exceptions. Many parties also agree express confidentiality terms.

Can an arbitration award be enforced in Portugal?

Both the United Kingdom and Portugal are parties to the New York Convention, so a foreign arbitral award can generally be recognised and enforced in either country, subject to the limited grounds for refusal set out in the Convention.

Do we need an arbitration clause to arbitrate?

Yes, there must be an agreement to arbitrate. If the contract does not contain one, the parties can still agree to arbitrate after the dispute arises.
