Many owners planning an extension in England or Wales only discover the Party Wall etc. Act 1996 when a neighbour mentions it — or when work has to stop. Planning early avoids most of the cost and delay.

## The three kinds of work the Act covers

1. **Building on the boundary.** A new wall at or astride the boundary line, such as the flank wall of an extension. Notice is required at least one month before work starts.
2. **Work to a shared (party) wall or structure.** Cutting in beams, underpinning, removing chimney breasts, raising or thickening a party wall. Notice is required at least two months before work starts.
3. **Excavating close to a neighbour's building.** Digging foundations or drainage trenches within 3 metres of a neighbour's structure and below their foundations — or within 6 metres where the dig cuts a 45° line down from their foundations. Notice is required at least one month before work starts.

The third category is the one most often overlooked. A rear extension with ordinary strip foundations can easily fall within it.

## What happens after notice is served

The adjoining owner can consent in writing or dissent. If they do not reply within 14 days, the owners are treated as being in dispute. Surveyors must then be appointed — either one *agreed surveyor* acting for both, or one surveyor each — and they agree a **Party Wall Award** setting out how and when the work can be done.

Although the Act does not say so expressly, the surveyors will usually award that their reasonable fees are paid by the owner carrying out the work.

## Five practical steps for building owners

- **Start early.** Notice periods are one or two months, and complex awards can take longer.
- **Talk to your neighbours before the formal notice arrives.** Neighbours who feel informed are far less likely to dissent straight away.
- **Have your plans checked** by an experienced party wall surveyor to confirm whether the Act applies and which notices are needed.
- **Ask about an agreed surveyor.** If your neighbour accepts the surveyor who drafted your notice as the agreed surveyor, costs on small projects stay down.
- **Record the condition of the neighbouring property** before work begins, so any damage can be fairly identified — and any unfounded claim answered.
